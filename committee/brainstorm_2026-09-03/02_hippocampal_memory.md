## 1. The core thesis (why the hippocampal lens is the *rival* argument, not just flavor)

The Transformer's KV cache is a hippocampus that **never consolidates**. It stores every token,
losslessly, forever, at O(t) memory — biologically absurd and economically the thing that
dominates long-context serving cost (the repo's own 28–455× memory analysis). The brain's answer
to the same problem is CLS: a fast, sparse, episodic store (hippocampus) that supports one-trial
learning, periodically replayed to a slow overlapping store (neocortex) that generalizes, after
which the episodic trace can decay. **Prizma-Seq already has the fast store (S) and Prizma-CL
already has the slow store (frozen experts); what is missing is the replay bridge and the decay.**
Completing the loop is simultaneously (a) the strongest brain-likeness claim available to this
project and (b) a concrete engineering differentiator no KV-cache architecture can copy without
abandoning its own design.

Ranked proposals follow. IMPACT/FEASIBILITY are 1–10; borrowed items carry citations.

## 2. Proposals (ranked by impact)

### P1. Replay as systems consolidation: drain S into slow weights, then decay S ("the CLS loop")

**WHAT.** Give Prizma-Seq (and later the unified Prizma) a consolidation phase: during training,
at intervals, sample pseudo-items from the *current state* `S` (its key/value associations,
generated by querying S with stored keys or random probes → reconstructed values), train the
FFN/slow weights on these pseudo-pairs with the ordinary loss, then apply a mild extra decay to
`S`'s forget gate for consolidated content. Over a document, episodic detail migrates from S to
weights; S's capacity is continuously freed for *new* episodes.

**WHY (brain).** This is verbatim CLS (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995; Kumaran, Hassabis &
McClelland 2016): hippocampal rapid storage + neocortical slow learning driven by hippocampal
replay (Wilson & McNaughton 1994; Ólafsdóttir et al. 2018). Hippocampal replay is exactly
"generate pseudo-items from a fast store to train a slow store." Place-cell replay even
reconstructs trajectories in compressed forward/reverse sweeps — supporting S-probe-based
generation rather than a raw experience buffer.

**WHY (transformer-rival).** The Transformer has no mechanism for this: its "episodic" KV cache
can only grow. Prizma-Seq with consolidation would claim: *constant memory at inference AND
retained knowledge* — knowledge that outlives the state that learned it. This directly attacks
the one axis where transformers are unbeatable (unbounded exact recall) by making it unnecessary
for the actual task (LM: most context matters briefly, then should become "knowledge").

**HOW.** New module alongside `seq/prizma_seq.py`'s training loop (e.g. a `consolidate()`
call in `common.py`'s train loop, off by default): (1) probe buffer = last W window keys or
random unit queries; (2) targets = `S_probe` reconstructions, gated by reconstruction confidence
(only replay high-confidence completions — hippocampal replay is selectively reactivated);
(3) FFN trains on pseudo-items at a small LR; (4) state decay multiplier applied per consolidated
association. Then the flagship experiment: **continual char-LM** — corpus A, then corpus B,
never re-exposing A; measure A-test BPC after B with and without the consolidation loop, vs a
Transformer that must keep A's KV or refit A into weights by re-reading.

**FALSIFIABLE TEST (pre-register).** Bar: on text8→tiny-shakespeare sequential continual LM,
Prizma+CLS-loop retains A-test BPC within +0.10 of its pre-B value while Prizma-without-loop
degrades by ≥0.25 and the state-only model's recall of *specific* A n-grams (probe task) drops
below 0.5 — i.e., consolidation demonstrably moved something into weights. If A-retention gain
<0.05, or perplexity on B worsens >0.05 (interference), the mechanism FAILS and is reported.

**RISKS.** Replay-from-S may generate garbage (low-rank S reconstructs poorly) → confidence gate
is essential; pseudo-item training may be optimization-harmful; adds train-time cost (already ~5×
slower — mitigate: replay every K steps, budget disclosed); the honest objection "replay ≈ an
experience buffer in disguise" must be answered by keeping the buffer *closed* (only
S-generated items, never raw data) and disclosed in the borrowed-vs-new ledger.

**IMPACT 10 · FEASIBILITY 5.** Borrowed: CLS theory (McClelland et al. 1995), replay-to-cortex
(Ólafsdóttir 2018), generative-replay heritage (Shin et al. 2017, Deep Generative Replay — must
be cited as prior art; novelty here is *state-generated* replay inside a delta-state mixer, which
is new). This is the single most "Prizma-brain" move available.

### P2. One-trial episodic binding benchmark: test whether the delta write already *is* one-shot hippocampal learning

**WHAT.** The delta rule writes `u_t = β_t(v_t − S_{t−1}k_t)` — error-proportional, i.e. a novel
pair (large error) is written near-fully in ONE step, a known pair (small error) barely changes S.
That is a mathematical statement of hippocampal one-trial learning with no retraining. But **the
repo has never measured it as a property.** Add an *episodic insertion test*: at test time,
inject a new key→value binding into a running context once (mid-sequence), query it at varying
distances, with distractors trained to collide (similar keys).

**WHY (brain).** Hippocampus is defined by one-shot episodic encoding with high capacity for
arbitrary conjunctions (O'Reilly & Norman 2002 — hippocampal "avoiding catastrophe" via sparse
pattern separation). MQAR measures *within-context* recall after training; episodic memory's
signature is *insertion-time* encoding without any gradient step on data.

**WHY (rival).** A Transformer must attend back to the raw token (KV must persist). If Prizma's S
truly binds one-trial, Prizma can answer cued recall of an item whose KV-relevant tokens could
in principle be dropped — a qualitative claim: *memory is in the state, not the cache*. Combined
with P1's decay it yields a staged memory story (episodic → consolidated) that attention cannot
tell at all.

**HOW.** New task in `seq/tasks.py`: `EpisodicMQAR` — training corpus has D pairs as usual; eval
splits into (a) trained bindings (tests consolidation via weights) and (b) held-out bindings
inserted at a marked point of the eval context only (tests one-trial state encoding). Report
accuracy vs distance, vs number of distractor colliders, and vs the pre-registered D\*.

**FALSIFIABLE TEST.** Pre-register: one-trial binding accuracy ≥0.9 at distance ≤1k for
`quad2, d_φ=256, d_h=32` with ≤8 colliding distractors; degrades to ≤0.5 with ≥32 colliders
(pattern separation limit = DG homologue's known sparse-code tradeoff). If one-trial accuracy ≈
multi-step-trained accuracy (i.e., the delta gate doesn't distinguish novel vs familiar), the
"fast-binding" claim is refuted and the write gate β_t should be re-examined (see P6).

**RISKS.** Likely the model *does* pass trivially (delta rule is designed for this) — but an
unmeasured claim is not a claim, and the distractor-collision axis is where it can genuinely
fail; also eval-only bindings interact with the window head (control: window-off arm).

**IMPACT 8 · FEASIBILITY 8.** Borrowed: one-trial learning framing (O'Reilly & Norman 2002),
MQAR scaffold (Zoology/Based). New: the insertion-protocol and the trained-vs-injected binding
dissociation.

### P3. DG-like pattern separation as a *controlled dial*, and a pre-registered separation/completion curve

**WHAT.** Treat `quad2` explicitly as the DG excitatory-granule-cell expansion (roll: d_h → d_φ
monomials = high-dimensional recoding; sparsification = the `_l2`-normalized monomial vector's
effective sparsity). Make separation/completion a *measured tradeoff curve*, not a side effect:
sweep (a) `d_φ` (expansion size ∝ DG scaling), (b) an output sparsification temperature on φ
(top-k monomial gating — a k-winner "granule cell" nonlinearity, 0 params if hard-wired),
(c) the forget-gate α floor (CA3 decay). For each point, measure BOTH crosstalk (separation,
already instrumented: `key_crosstalk`) and completion accuracy under key corruption
(completion). Plot the empirical PS/PC frontier.

**WHY (brain).** DG pattern separation and CA3 pattern completion are in measured tension
in vivo: novelty/tasks shift the circuit toward separation (DG), familiarity toward completion
(CA3; see Guo, Huszár etc.; canonical reviews: Rolls & Treves 1994; Yassa & Stark 2011 — the
"baker/bakers" bias). A single architecture exhibiting the *frontier* — and a knob that moves
along it — is a far stronger brain-likeness claim than any one operating point.

**WHY (rival).** The frontier's endpoints are engineering numbers: separation limit = max D at
fixed memory (MQAR D\*), completion limit = robust recall under corruption (serving-grade
retrieval). Both matter for the alternative-architecture case; currently only D\* is measured.

**HOW.** `feat_n2` sweep exists; add (i) a `topk_phi` config knob in `PrizmaSeqConfig` (hard
k-winner on the monomial vector, k as fraction of d_φ — zero trainable params, seeded like the
monomials), (ii) a corruption task (`noisy-key MQAR`: eval keys perturbed by ε; accuracy vs ε =
completion curve), (iii) reuse crosstalk as the separation axis. O(1) guard (`step==forward`)
must stay green with topk (it is elementwise on φ, so it will).

**FALSIFIABLE TEST.** Pre-register: (1) the frontier is monotone — no config dominates both axes
(by >0.03 on both); (2) topk_phi=0.3 improves crosstalk ≥20% relative at ≤0.02 completion cost
at D=128; (3) Gershgorin cross(φ) under topk predicts the measured D\* within a factor 1.5
(connecting the `docs/quad2_theoretical_convergence.md` capacity math to the DG story). Failure
of (1) is itself a reportable surprise (a free lunch would be a finding, not a loss).

**RISKS.** Topk breaks smoothness (training gradient through hard-k needs straight-through or
subgradient — must disclose); scope creep into a tuning carnival — keep it a fixed 3-axis grid,
pre-registered, one seed for the frontier map + 5 seeds on endpoints only.

**IMPACT 7 · FEASIBILITY 7.** Borrowed: DG/CA3 segregation (Rolls & Treves 1994; Yassa & Stark
2011), k-WTA sparseness literature. New: measuring the PS/PC frontier on a delta-state mixer and
tying it to Gershgorin cross-talk bounds.

### P4. Consolidation-freeze → consolidation-*transfer* in Prizma-CL: replay the frozen expert before freezing (fixes the interleaving collapse?)

**WHAT.** Prizma-CL's known limit: interleaved streams collapse (ACC ~0.58) because the phase
detector needs temporal contiguity. Hippocampal answer: what consolidates is not a *block* but a
*conjunction tagged by temporal context*. Two coupled changes: (a) **context-tagged recognition**
— augment each expert's recon-surprise input with a slowly-drifting temporal-context signal
(random low-frequency projection of recent-input statistics, i.e. a "time cell" / context code);
(b) **replay-before-freeze** — on commit, run a short generative-replay pass (the frozen expert's
own decoder generates pseudo-inputs; the active expert's encoder/decoder is *not* touched, but a
small shared "cortical" readout is distilled across experts) so that committed knowledge also
lives in a slowly-updating shared component, per CLS.

**WHY (brain).** Hippocampal episodic memory binds items *in spatiotemporal context*
(Howard & Kahana temporal context models; time-cell sequences, Eichenbaum 2014); systems
consolidation makes traces cortically independent of the hippocampus even for interleaved
material. The current freeze is a wall — biologically it's a lesion (hippocampectomy at commit),
and it cannot survive interleaving because nothing is shared.

**WHY (rival/CL).** If context-tagging alone recovers interleaved-domain routing (the single
biggest honest failure in `docs/Prizma.md` §8), the domain-incremental assumption — the repo's
most binding limit — is materially weakened without any task label.

**HOW.** In `src/prizma.py`: add a `ctx` vector (EMA of input second-moments, dim d_ctx ≪ d) mixed
into `recon_error` at small weight; add `replay_consolidate(e, shared)` called at commit. Keep
fully-local: pseudo-inputs from the expert's own decoder, delta-rule distillation into the shared
readout (no backprop). Route ablations: ctx-off, replay-off, both-off (existing behavior).

**FALSIFIABLE TEST.** Pre-register: on the interleaved structured-permuted stream (10 seeds,
same S1–S6 CI discipline as E1), Prizma+ctx achieves FGT ≤ 0.15 and ACC ≥ 0.70 vs the published
~0.58/0.49-collapse; ablation shows ctx is causal (ctx-off reproduces the collapse within CI).
If interleaved ACC stays ≤0.65, the mechanism fails — reported as such, like E5.

**RISKS.** A drifting context signal can *invent* boundaries (premature commits) — needs the
same precision-test discipline as the existing detector; generative replay from a shallow AE may
produce weak pseudo-inputs (cite Shin et al. 2017 as prior art, honestly); this touches the
repo's flagship, so all changes must be default-off, new experiment IDs (E6+), full referee round.

**IMPACT 9 · FEASIBILITY 5.** Borrowed: temporal context models (Howard & Kahana 2002), time
cells (Eichenbaum 2014), generative replay (Shin 2017). New: surprise-context joint vigilance in
a fully-local expert mixture.

### P5. Adult-neurogenesis dynamics: young-expert hyperplasticity + granule-cell allocation

**WHAT.** In `Prizma.train_batch`, a recruited expert currently behaves like an adult one (same
LR, same vigilance). Give newly-recruited experts a **young period**: elevated LR, elevated
vigilance (finer separation, like new granule cells which are more excitable and more
separation-biased), decaying over `n_seen` with a fixed schedule — mirroring the validated
developmental finding that young granule cells are hyperplastic and preferentially recruited for
novel-pattern learning (Ge, Yang, Ming & Song 2007; commonly modeled in DG allocation work:
Aimone, Wiles & Gage 2009; available-cells allocation models, Mesa et al.).

**WHY (brain).** Direct homolog: neurogenesis = a pool of uninitialized experts; allocation = ART
recruitment; critical period = young-LR schedule. This is the cheapest high-fidelity mapping in
the whole project — it changes two scalars in an existing, tested file.

**WHY (rival/CL).** Fixable weakness: hard-commit cascade and vigilance thrashing were both
observed in the iteration log (`docs/Prizma.md` §7 v2). A young-period schedule is a principled
prior over the commit decision (young experts are allowed to be wrong → later committed with
better precision estimates), addressing the *process* failures documented in v2–v4.

**HOW.** In `src/prizma.py`: `lr_eff = lr * (1 + γ·exp(−n_seen/τ))`, `z_novel_eff = z_novel·(1−δ·exp(...))`,
two new hyperparameters, default-off byte-identical. Sweep γ, τ on E1 and the interleaved E6.

**FALSIFIABLE TEST.** Pre-register: E1 ACC improves ≥+0.02 (or commit-count variance across seeds
drops ≥30%) without FGT > 0.01; if neither, FAIL (it is a dynamics tweak — impact lives or dies
on the measured commit stability).

**RISKS.** Smell risk: "two more hyperparameters, dressed in neurons." Honest defense is the
pre-registered narrow bar and the borrowed-vs-new row marked *borrowed-mechanism, new-context*.
Low ceiling on impact alone; scores high as infrastructure for P4's interleaving fix.

**IMPACT 5 · FEASIBILITY 9.** Borrowed: Ge et al. 2007; Aimone et al. 2009. New: application to
vigilance-routed expert pools.

### P6. CA3 autoassociation gate: make the write gate genuinely surprise-modulated (the owed powered ablation)

**WHAT.** The borrowed-vs-new ledger honestly records that the precision/surprise gate is
**asserted, not demonstrated**, and the only ablation (n=2 smoke) *points against* it
(constant 0.566 > real signal 0.517). From the hippocampal side, the correct mechanism is not
"surprise scales the write" — it's the CA3 criteria: **write only on novelty, protect on
familiarity** (LTP/LTD-like, absysical "elastic weight" behavior of episodic store). Design the
decisive powered ablation: gate arms = {constant, random, surprise-norm (current),
*familiarity-suppressed* (β_t = σ(−‖ε‖² + b), i.e., write LESS as S already predicts — the
inverse of current), oracle-difficulty-gated}. n≥10, full LR protocol, MQAR D=128 + one
episodic-insertion leg (P2's task).

**WHY (brain).** CA3 autoassociation is bidirectionally plastic with novelty/familiarity
modulation (Vogt & Canepari; DG novelty signals via mossy cells). The *sign* of the
surprise-gate matters biologically; the current `surprise_norm` may be the wrong sign, which
would *explain* why it underperforms a constant gate — that's a hypothesis, not an excuse; the
experiment adjudicates.

**WHY (rival).** If any surprise-modulated arm beats constant at power, the repo's "new" novelty
line flips from refuted-leaning to demonstrated — with a causal ablation under repo-grade rigor.
If all surprise arms ≤ constant, the ledger row should be honestly retired and the free-energy
framing kept as derivation-only. Either outcome strengthens the project.

**HOW.** `results/gpu_ablation.json` harness extended: arms above, n=10, the fair protocol from
`common.py`, TOST-style verdict layer like `landscape.py`. ~1 GPU-day.

**FALSIFIABLE TEST.** Pre-register: claim "surprise-gating helps" iff surprise-norm (or
familiarity-suppressed) beats constant-gate with non-overlapping 95% CI on MQAR D=128 solve-rate
AND on episodic-insertion one-trial accuracy. Everything else = "no demonstrated benefit," and
the README novelty row is downgraded.

**RISKS.** Risk of confirming the negative — which is fine under this repo's culture (see B4's
PARTIAL handling). Compute is modest; the reputational payoff of settling an honesty-owed
ablation is large.

**IMPACT 8 · FEASIBILITY 8.** Borrowed: novelty modulation of hippocampal plasticity (Li, McNally
reviews). New: sign-resolved surprise gating in delta-state writers.

### P7. Hippocampal capacity theory vs the N<14 wall: remapping, not just expansion

**WHAT.** The Gershgorin bound `N < 1 + 1/cross(φ)` gives capacity ≈14 for quad2 keys — a wall
currently escaped only by growing `d_φ`. Biology escapes capacity walls differently: **global
remapping** — the *same* circuit re-randomizes its code per context (Muller & Kubie; Leutgeb &
Leutgeb), decorrelating interference without more neurons. Mechanism here: per-layer (or
per-head-group) **independent seeded monomial sets** for φ (already seeded buffers — make the
seeds differ across heads/layers and, in a variant, re-seed per token-block with a public
block-index, i.e., a hash-based "context remap"). Remapped sub-states interfere quasi-orthogonally
→ effective capacity ≈ sum over remaps at the same param count, still O(1) state (state dims
unchanged; the *values* written are decorrelated).

**WHY (brain).** Remapping is THE hippocampal capacity trick (Leutgeb et al. 2007: DG rate
remapping supports pattern separation across environments).

**WHY (rival).** Capacity per constant memory is the Pareto axis where Prizma fights transformers
(round1 §B: D\* ≈ 1.0·d_h per state). Remapping could multiply effective D\* without touching
params or inference cost — cheaper than lever #2/#3 in round1 §C.

**HOW.** Config `phi_remap ∈ {static (current), per-head, per-block}`; `per-block`: φ_seed =
hash(base_seed, block_idx); keys/queries remapped consistently within a block; cross-block reads
handled by the window head (disclosed as a scope limit, mirroring episodic vs semantic memory:
state holds the current context's episodes; older contexts live in weights via P1 — the two
proposals compose into a real CLS story).

**FALSIFIABLE TEST.** Pre-register: per-block remap raises D\* (0.97 recall) from ~32 to ≥48 at
(d_h=32, d_φ=256, H=2) on within-block MQAR, with cross-block binding accuracy ≥0.9 for the
immediately previous block only (measured, disclosed decay). If D\* gain <1.3×, FAIL.

**RISKS.** Remapping breaks long-range recall (by design — that's the episodic/semantic split,
but it must be stated as a capability trade, not a free win); interaction with chunked-parallel
kernel (remap must be chunk-aligned); hash-φ is exotic — needs the rand_linear-style negative
control (remap with random-but-shared φ per block must show the same D\* gain if the gain is
just decorrelation — the control decides whether "remapping" adds anything beyond quad2).

**IMPACT 7 · FEASIBILITY 6.** Borrowed: remapping (Leutgeb et al. 2007; Muller & Kubie 1987).
New: hash-reseeded feature maps as capacity lever in a delta-state mixer.

### P8. The unified "Prizma brain" — explicit target architecture (not a proposal to build today, a proposal to commit to)

**WHAT.** A three-timescale composite: (1) **hippocampus** = Prizma-Seq carried state S
(one-trial, per-context, decaying); (2) **cortex** = Prizma-CL expert mixture (slow, local,
routing, freezing); (3) **replay bridge** = P1's state-generated consolidation loop + P4's
replay-before-freeze; (4) **novelty gate** = shared vigilance signal (ART + hippocampal
novelty detection) that drives routing (cortex), write gating (hippocampus), and replay
scheduling — the repo's existing "one surprise signal, two timescales" synthesis extended to a
third consumer. S sits *between* input and experts: experts consume S-reconstructions as their
input slice; S's write gate receives the winning expert's surprise.

**WHY (brain).** This is textbook CLS with the hippocampus as a content-addressable fast
autoassociator in series with cortical modules, unified by neuromodulatory surprise (acetylcholine
novelty signals driving hippocampal LTP and cortical plasticity, Hasselmo; Gu 2021 review).

**WHY (rival).** It is the only framing under which both existing threads stop being
small-scale curiosities and become one claim: *a system that learns continually, locally, with
constant episodic memory, because its memory has stages* — the property the KV-cache
architecture structurally lacks.

**HOW.** A design doc + a minimal E0 wedge (not full fusion): Prizma-CL's encoder replaced by a
one-layer Prizma-Seq block so experts get a state-mediated input slice; measure E1 protocol
parity first. No headline claims until the wedge reproduces E1 within CI.

**FALSIFIABLE TEST.** Bar: the wedge matches E1 (ACC within CI of 0.834, FGT 0) — fusion that
degrades the flagship is rejected (pre-registered kill criterion). Only then E6 (interleaved)
and the continual-LM flagship from P1.

**RISKS.** Highest complexity in the whole brainstorm; easy to produce a worse version of both
threads; must be sequenced *after* P1/P4 individually land; compute-bound. This is the capstone,
not the next step.

**IMPACT 9 · FEASIBILITY 3.** Borrowed: CLS (McClelland et al. 1995), Hasselmo cholinergic
modulation, ART (Carpenter & Grossberg). New: the composition and its surprise-unified control.

## 3. Deliberately rejected / deferred ideas (honesty)

- **Unbounded episodic key-value index with kNN retrieval** (RAG-style "hippocampal indexing
  theory", Teyler & Rudy 2007): refutes the O(1) differentiator for a capability transformers
  already own. Rejected as a rival-axis; the P2 insertion protocol is the honest bounded version.
- **Grid-cell successor-representation HEAD** for Prizma-CL (Stachenfeld et al. 2017): attractive,
  but the current HEAD placeholder isn't load-bearing in the tested regime; park until the
  relational/structure benchmark exists (would be proposal #9 with more evidence).
- **Hopfield-modern (dense associative memory) swap for S**: denser memories exist, but this
  abandons the free-energy/delta derivation that is Prizma's actual new framing; note as prior
  art to cite, not to adopt.
